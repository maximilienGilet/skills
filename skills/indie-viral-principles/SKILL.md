# Indie Viral Principles

A compass, not a checklist. Use the principles to orient judgment. Not every rule applies to every offer. Pick the ones that bite hardest for the situation, justify deviations, ignore the rest.

## Two modes

### Mode A: ANALYZE an existing offer

Goal: diagnose why an offer underperforms or could spread harder.

1. Read the offer end-to-end (URL, screenshots, copy, pricing, name)
2. Identify the single biggest leak (hero, paywall, pricing confusion, generic copy, no demo, etc.)
3. Pick the 3-7 principles that bear on the diagnosis. Quote the punchline.
4. For each: write what is currently there, why it leaks, and the concrete rewrite/redesign.
5. End with one sentence: the single change that would move the needle most.

### Mode B: GENERATE a new offer

Goal: shape a product/landing page/positioning that the principles would approve.

1. Ask (or infer): what problem, what wave, what audience, what unfair advantage of the founder?
2. Apply the principles as generative constraints, not as filters:
   - Name (23, 30) - one memorable word, known vocabulary
   - One-line pitch (7, 18, 30) - emotional, fifth-grader simple, under 10 words
   - Hero (20, 10, 3, 28) - sells alone, shows demo, numbers, CTA naming the next action
   - Outcome sold (24) - desire, not feature
   - Pricing shape (1, 8, 12, 27, 31) - hard paywall, popcorn tiers, one-time when possible, premium price
   - Wave to ride (13) - which trend/tech/topic carries the marketing
   - Founder angle (15, 9) - what only this founder can say or show
   - Surprise element (19) - the thing nobody has seen
3. Output the offer as a structured brief, not a webpage. Let downstream design skills (frontend-design, impeccable) handle visuals.

## The 31 principles

### Pricing and monetization (compass: charge more, sell less choice, ask early)

**1. No free plan.** Free users are leeches. <3% convert.
**8. Hard paywall.** Signups don't pay bills. Ask for payment before data.
**12. Popcorn Pricing.** Three tiers max: Good / Better / Best.
**16. Pricing in the header.** Pricing is part of the pitch.
**27. No subscription.** One-time payments are 10x easier to sell.
**31. More expensive than competitors.** Nobody talks about the second cheapest.

### Visual design (compass: silence is loud)

**2. Three colors max.** Black text, white background, one color for the Buy button.
**4. Shareable footer.** 97% won't buy, might share. Finish strong.
**5. OG image = YouTube thumbnail.** Seen more than the site itself.
**6. One idea per screen.** Like Instagram feed.

### Headline and copy (compass: feeling beats feature, statement beats estimate)

**3. Numbers, not adjectives.** "Save 4 hours every week" > "fast".
**7. Fifth-grader headline.** Mum should get it.
**9. Copy only you could write.** If a competitor can paste it, it is generic.
**14. Steal copy from customers.** They describe it better than you do.
**17. Memorable next day.** Write 5, show friends, ask 24h later, keep the sticky one.
**18. Emotional headline.** Laugh, wow, or WTF.
**20. Sell from hero alone.** 80% never scroll past.
**21. Empathy before sell.** Describe their problem better than they can.
**26. No weak words.** Ban "most", "many", "rarely". Statements, not estimates.
**28. CTA names the next action.** "Analyze My Website" > "Get Started".
**30. Under 10 words.** If you can't, users can't either.

### Product strategy (compass: one thing, one wave, one face, one CTA)

**10. Show before explain.** Demo > paragraphs.
**11. Do one thing.** Nobody remembers Swiss Army knives.
**13. Ride a wave.** The wave does half the marketing.
**15. Visible founder.** Founder face > corporate video.
**19. Something new.** Nobody shares another clone.
**22. One CTA.** Multiple paths = no path.
**23. Memorable name.** Words people know. No wordplay or made-up words.
**24. Sell desire, not feature.** Money, time, health, status, less pain.
**25. Let them try before buying.** Best features on the landing page.
**29. Testimonials before launch.** Proof before traffic.

## How to use the compass

- **Bite, don't blanket.** Quote only the 3-7 principles relevant to the current decision. A wall of 31 violations is noise.
- **Justify the exceptions.** If the product genuinely needs a subscription (27) or a free tier (1), say why. The compass points; the founder decides.
- **Trade-offs are real.** Empathy before sell (21) can fight sell from hero alone (20). Pick which one this offer needs more.
- **One-shot ranking.** When asked "what is the one thing to change", rank candidates by impact x ease and name a single move.

  Fix: <concrete rewrite/change>
